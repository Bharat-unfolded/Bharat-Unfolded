Bharat Unfolded · Bihar

History of Bihar

Ancient empires, the birth of major religions, great centres of learning and a defining role in India’s freedom struggle.

Ganga River at sunset, Patna · Photo: Prabhat114 · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0
Bihar is one of India's oldest and most historically significant regions. The name "Bihar" is derived from the Sanskrit and Pali word Vihara, meaning a Buddhist monastery. This land has served as the cradle of India’s first great empires, the birthplace of major world religions, the site of the world’s earliest republic, and a global hub of ancient higher education.
01 · Ancient

Ancient Period

  • Vedic Era and Mahajanapadas: In ancient times, Bihar was the center of three major kingdoms (Mahajanapadas): Magadha, Anga, and Vajji (Mithila/Vaishali). The Licchavi Republic of Vaishali is widely recognized as one of the world’s earliest republics.
  • Rise of Buddhism and Jainism: In the 6th century BCE, Bihar became the spiritual epicenter of two major religions. Gautama Buddha attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, while Lord Mahavira, the 24th Jain Tirthankara, was born in Kundagram (Vaishali) and attained Nirvana at Pawapuri.
  • The Rise of Magadha and the Mauryan Empire: Following the Haryanka, Shishunaga, and Nanda dynasties, Chandragupta Maurya—guided by his chief advisor Chanakya—established the Mauryan Empire with Pataliputra (modern-day Patna) as its capital, uniting most of the Indian subcontinent. His grandson, Emperor Ashoka, embraced Buddhism after the Kalinga War and spread its message of peace across Asia.
  • The Gupta Empire (Golden Age): During the 4th and 5th centuries CE, under rulers like Samudragupta and Chandragupta II, Pataliputra once again flourished as a global center for science, mathematics (notably Aryabhata), literature, and art.
  • World-Renowned Centers of Learning: Established during the Gupta period, Nalanda University, along with Vikramshila University (founded by Pala King Dharmapala), attracted scholars and students from across Asia.
02 · Medieval

Medieval Period

  • Pala and Karnat Dynasties: From the 8th to the 12th centuries, the Pala rulers patronized Buddhist art and education in southern Bihar, while the Karnat dynasty ruled northern Bihar (Mithila).
  • Turkic Invasions and the Delhi Sultanate: At the end of the 12th century, invasions led by Bakhtiyar Khilji caused severe destruction to Nalanda and Vikramshila, bringing Bihar under the control of the Delhi Sultanate.
  • Rule of Sher Shah Suri: In the 16th century, Sher Shah Suri of Sasaram defeated the Mughal Emperor Humayun to establish the Sur Empire. He rebuilt Patna, introduced major administrative and currency reforms (introducing the Rupiya), and modernized the Grand Trunk Road.
  • Mughal Era: Under Emperor Akbar, Bihar became a formal province (Subah) of the Mughal Empire. In 1666 CE, Guru Gobind Singh Ji, the tenth Sikh Guru, was born in Patna, making Takht Sri Patna Sahib one of the holiest sites in Sikhism.
03 · Modern

Modern Period & Freedom Struggle

  • Beginning of British Rule: Following victories at Plassey (1757) and especially the Battle of Buxar (1764), the British East India Company secured the Diwani (revenue collection rights) over Bihar, Bengal, and Orissa.
  • Revolt of 1857: Babu Veer Kunwar Singh, the 80-year-old Zamindar of Jagdishpur (Arrah), led a legendary and fierce armed resistance against British forces during India's First War of Independence.
  • Champaran Satyagraha (1917): Mahatma Gandhi launched his first successful Satyagraha in India in Bihar's Champaran district to protest the exploitation of indigo farmers, marking a turning point in India's freedom movement.
  • Key Freedom Fighters: Leaders from Bihar—including Dr. Rajendra Prasad, Jayaprakash Narayan (Loknayak), Dr. Sri Krishna Sinha, Anugrah Narayan Sinha, Maulana Mazharul Haque, and Swami Sahajanand Saraswati—played decisive roles in the Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience, and 1942 Quit India movements.
04 · Statehood

Formation of the State & Post-Independence

  • Separation from Bengal: On March 22, 1912, Bihar and Orissa were carved out of the Bengal Presidency as a separate province (celebrated annually as Bihar Diwas).
  • Reorganizations of 1936 and 2000: Orissa was separated from Bihar in 1936. Later, on November 15, 2000, the southern region of Bihar was bifurcated to create the new state of Jharkhand.
  • Role in Independent India: Bihar gave independent India its first President, Dr. Rajendra Prasad. In 1974, Jayaprakash Narayan led the historic Sampoorna Kranti (Total Revolution) movement from Patna, reshaping modern Indian politics.

01 · NALANDA, BIHAR

Nalanda University

Nalanda Mahavihara was established under Emperor Kumaragupta I in the 5th century CE. From the 5th to the 12th century, it grew into one of the most celebrated centres of learning in the world. Students travelled here from China, Mongolia, Tibet, Korea and other parts of Asia because of its international reputation.

The standard of teaching was exceptionally high. Students seeking admission were tested by teachers before they could study at the Mahavihara. Nalanda was a leading centre for Mahayana Buddhist learning, while subjects beyond Buddhism were also taught here.

At the end of the 12th century, the institution suffered destruction during the invasion led by Bakhtiyar Khilji. Monks were killed and the valuable library was burned. Today, Nalanda is remembered as a symbol of Asian intellectual exchange, and Nalanda University has been established nearby.

02 · NALANDA, BIHAR

Hiuen Tsang Memorial Hall

This memorial honours Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang), the 7th-century Chinese Buddhist monk and scholar who studied and taught at ancient Nalanda. Proposed to strengthen India–China cultural ties, its construction began in 1960 and was completed in 1984.

The Chinese-style complex features a statue of Xuanzang, a memorial column, a bell enclosure and artworks depicting his travels and Buddhist history. Allow around 45–90 minutes for a visit. Please keep the hall quiet and do not touch exhibits or artwork.

03 · BODH GAYA, GAYA, BIHAR

Mahabodhi Temple

Mahabodhi Temple in Bodh Gaya marks the place where Gautama Buddha attained enlightenment beneath the Bodhi Tree. Emperor Ashoka built an early shrine and the Vajrasana (Diamond Throne) here in the 3rd century BCE. The grand brick temple seen today largely dates to the 5th–6th centuries CE.

After centuries of repairs and periods of neglect, the complex underwent major restoration by Burmese rulers and British archaeologists in the 19th century, including work in the 1880s. Conservation and management later came under the Bodh Gaya Temple Act of 1949. The site is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

04 · GAYA, BIHAR

Shri Vishnupad Temple

Standing on the banks of the Falgu River in Gaya, Shri Vishnupad Temple is a major Hindu pilgrimage site, especially for Pind Daan and ancestral rites. Its sanctum holds a roughly 40 cm footprint, traditionally believed to be Lord Vishnu’s, set in rock within a silver octagonal basin. A pillared sabha mandap and tall pyramidal spire form part of the temple complex.

The present temple is widely credited to Queen Ahilyabai Holkar of Indore, who rebuilt or restored it in 1787 on an older sacred site. Pilgrims visit throughout the year, with especially large numbers during Pitru Paksha.

05 · RAJGIR, NALANDA, BIHAR

Vishwa Shanti Stupa

The Vishwa Shanti Stupa (World Peace Pagoda) stands on Ratnagiri Hill in Rajgir. Built by the Japanese Buddhist organization Nipponzan-Myohoji and inaugurated in 1969, the white marble pagoda has four golden Buddha statues representing birth, enlightenment, preaching and nirvana. The hilltop offers panoramic views of the Rajgir valley.

06 · RAJGIR, NALANDA, BIHAR

Griddhakuta Peak (Vulture’s Peak)

Griddhakuta Peak lies in the hills of ancient Rajgir. Its name means Vulture’s Peak, inspired by a distinctive rock formation resembling a vulture. Buddhist tradition remembers this as a place where Gautama Buddha spent rainy seasons meditating and teaching, with the Lotus Sutra and Heart Sutra associated with the site in Buddhist tradition.

The hill has ancient stone steps, rock-cut meditation caves and a small summit shrine. The climb is best planned in the cooler morning or late afternoon.

07 · RAJGIR, NALANDA, BIHAR

Brahm Kund

Brahm Kund is a sacred hot-spring complex in Rajgir, fed by seven underground streams. The main pool is known for its naturally warm water, reaching around 45°C. The springs are revered in Hindu, Buddhist and Jain traditions, and the stepped bathing kunds include separate areas for men and women.

Venu Van (Venuvana Vihara)

Venu Van is a peaceful bamboo grove and monastery complex near Brahm Kund. According to Buddhist tradition, King Bimbisara gifted this bamboo park to Gautama Buddha as an early monastic residence. The grounds include remains of a brick stupa, the central Kalandaka Tank and shaded walking paths for quiet walks and meditation.

09 · RAJGIR, NALANDA, BIHAR

Ghora Katora Lake

Ghora Katora is a scenic natural lake in a bowl-shaped valley surrounded by hills near Rajgir. Its name means “horse bowl,” and the lake features a large Buddha statue rising from the water. The quiet setting is popular for its hill views and boating.

Fuel-powered motor vehicles are restricted on the inner approach. Visitors usually continue from the drop-off point by e-rickshaw, tonga or bicycle.

12 · PATNA, BIHAR

Buddha Smriti Park (Buddha Memorial Park)

Located on Fraser Road opposite Patna Junction, this 22-acre memorial park was built on the former site of Bankipur Central Jail and inaugurated by the 14th Dalai Lama on 27 May 2010. Its centrepiece is the 200-foot Patliputra Karuna Stupa, which houses sacred Buddha relics excavated at Vaishali. The park also features Bodhi tree saplings from Bodh Gaya and Anuradhapura, international votive stupas, landscaped gardens, a meditation centre, library and multimedia museum.

14 · PATNA, BIHAR

Golghar

Near Gandhi Maidan and the Ganga, Golghar is a beehive-shaped granary built in 1786 by Captain John Garstin for the British Army after the devastating famine of 1770. The 29-metre-high structure has no internal pillars and is known for its exterior spiral staircase of 145 steps, originally designed for carrying grain. Its grounds and elevated viewpoint offer views of the river and Patna skyline.

15 · VAISHALI, BIHAR

Buddha Relic Stupa

This ancient mud-and-brick stupa at Vaishali is traditionally associated with the Licchavis, who are said to have received one of the eight portions of the Buddha’s relics after his Mahaparinirvana. The archaeological remains were excavated between 1958 and 1962. The nearby Buddha Samyak Darshan Museum displays the sacred relics and explains Vaishali’s Buddhist heritage.

18 · ANTICHAK, BHAGALPUR, BIHAR

Vikramshila University Ruins

Founded in the late 8th century CE by Pala Emperor Dharmapala, Vikramshila was one of ancient India’s leading Buddhist learning centres and flourished for around four centuries. It became renowned for Vajrayana Buddhism, philosophy, logic, grammar and metaphysics, with the scholar Atisha Dipankara among its best-known figures. The university was destroyed around the end of the 12th century. Excavations have revealed a vast square monastery with more than 200 monk cells, a central cruciform brick stupa decorated with terracotta plaques, Buddha chambers and remains of a library.

20 · SASARAM, ROHTAS, BIHAR

Tomb of Sher Shah Suri

Built between 1540 and 1545 CE in memory of Sher Shah Suri, founder of the Sur Empire, this 122-foot sandstone mausoleum stands in the middle of a large artificial lake. Designed by Mir Muhammad Aliwal Khan, its octagonal plan, grand dome, corner chhatris and stone causeway make it a celebrated example of Indo-Afghan architecture. The tomb was completed during the reign of Sher Shah’s son, Islam Shah.

21 · ROHTAS, BIHAR

Rohtasgarh Fort

Perched high on the Kaimur plateau above the Son valley, Rohtasgarh is an extensive hill fort with a long history and massive defensive walls. Sher Shah Suri captured it in 1539, and Raja Man Singh later expanded the complex during Akbar’s reign. Its historic remains include gateways such as Hathiya Pol, palace buildings, courtyards, mosques and temples. The fort is traditionally associated with Rohitaswa, son of King Harishchandra.

22 · BARABAR HILLS, JEHANABAD, BIHAR

Barabar Caves

Carved into massive granite hills during the Mauryan period in the 3rd century BCE, the Barabar Caves are among the oldest surviving rock-cut caves in India. Several caves were dedicated to Ajivika ascetics by Emperor Ashoka and his grandson Dasharatha. The best-known caves include Lomas Rishi, Sudama, Karan Chaupar and Vishwakarma. Their mirror-like polished interiors, inscriptions and distinctive rock-cut architecture make the site unique.

Buxar Fort

Standing near the southern bank of the Ganga in Buxar, this historic fort is traditionally dated to 1054 CE and associated with King Rudra Deo. The fort area preserves historic remains and riverfront views close to the town’s ghats.

24 · WEST CHAMPARAN, BIHAR

Valmiki Tiger Reserve

Along Bihar’s Indo-Nepal border, Valmiki Tiger Reserve is the state’s only tiger reserve and forms part of the Himalayan Terai forest landscape adjoining Nepal’s Chitwan National Park. Its sal forests, grasslands and river valleys shelter Bengal tigers, leopards, sloth bears, elephants, deer and rich birdlife. The main visitor zones include Valmikinagar, Manguraha and Gobardhana.

25 · GAYA, BIHAR

Maa Sarva Mangla Gauri Shakti Peeth Temple

Located atop Mangla Gauri Hill, also known in tradition as Bhasmakoot Parvat, this temple is revered as one of the sacred Shakti Peethas. Hindu belief holds that a part of Goddess Sati fell at this site. The shrine is associated with maternal protection, nourishment and auspiciousness, and is especially visited on Tuesdays and during Navratri.

The hilltop temple features stone architecture and carvings associated with medieval construction, along with earlier sculptural remains. Visitors reach the shrine by climbing around 100–120 stone steps. The compound also contains smaller shrines and offers views over Gaya.

26 · PATNA, BIHAR

Mahavir Mandir (Patna)

Located on Fraser Road beside Patna Junction, Mahavir Mandir is one of the most visited temples dedicated to Lord Hanuman in Bihar and northern India. The temple is known for its distinctive pair of Hanuman idols in the sanctum, representing different forms of the deity.

The temple's history is traditionally traced to an earlier shrine associated with the Ramanandi tradition. The present structure was renovated in the 1980s under the guidance of Acharya Kishore Kunal and the Mahavir Mandir Trust. Other shrines in the complex include those dedicated to Lord Shiva, Lord Rama, Sita and Lakshman.

30 · PAWAPURI, NALANDA, BIHAR

Jal Mandir, Pawapuri

Located in Pawapuri, Jal Mandir is one of Jainism’s holiest pilgrimage sites. The white-marble shrine stands on an island platform in the middle of a large lotus tank and is reached by a stone causeway. Jain tradition holds that Lord Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara, attained Nirvana and was cremated at Pawapuri.

The temple’s sanctum venerates the sacred charan paduka (footprints) of Lord Mahavira. According to tradition, devotees carried away soil from the cremation site, gradually forming the depression that became the water tank. The peaceful pond, lotus blooms and marble shrine make this an important destination, especially during Diwali and Mahavir Jayanti.
