India Gate: A Memorial in the Heart of Delhi

A monumental arch, a tribute to soldiers, and one of the capital's best-known public spaces.

At the heart of New Delhi stands India Gate, a 42-metre-high memorial arch set amid a broad road junction. Its design recalls the Arc de Triomphe in Paris. The monument commemorates around 70,000 Indian soldiers who lost their lives while serving in the British Indian Army during the First World War. It also carries the names of more than 13,516 British and Indian soldiers who died on the North-West Frontier during the Afghan War of 1919.

Foundation and dedication

The foundation stone was laid in 1921 by His Royal Highness the Duke of Connaught. The memorial was designed by architect Edwin Lutyens and was dedicated to the nation ten years later by the Viceroy, Lord Irwin. After India's independence, the site gained another memorial element: the Amar Jawan Jyoti, an eternal flame honouring soldiers who gave their lives in the Indo-Pakistan War of December 1971.

Architecture and inscriptions

The arch rises from a low base built with red Bharatpur stone, with its structure stepping upward towards a large moulded section. Imperial suns appear on the cornice. On either side of the arch, the word “INDIA” is accompanied by the Roman-numeral dates MCMXIV (1914) and MCMXIX (1919). A shallow domed bowl at the top was originally intended to hold a ceremonial flame on anniversaries, although this is rarely done.

Evenings around India Gate

After sunset, floodlighting gives the sandstone monument a dramatic appearance. Nearby fountains add colour to the evening scene. India Gate stands at one end of the ceremonial avenue, and the surrounding neighbourhood is commonly known by the same name.

A wide stretch of green lawns surrounds the memorial and has long been a popular place for picnics and relaxed walks. On summer evenings, visitors gather across the lawns and around the brightly lit monument, making the area one of Delhi's familiar public meeting places.
