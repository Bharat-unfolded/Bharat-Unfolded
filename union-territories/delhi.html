33 Incredible Tourist Places to Visit in Delhi

Capital of India, the historical and vivacious Delhi is blessed with innumerable monuments, scenic locations and prominent shrines. From historic buildings showcasing the Mughal Era and British Raj to some of the country's best markets, entertainment zones and cuisines, Delhi has something for everyone.

Delhi also boasts art galleries, rich museums and educational institutions. This metropolitan city brings together the modern and ancient worlds. Explore the popular sightseeing locations below.

01 / DELHI

Qutub Minar

A victory tower, located within the premises of Qutub Complex, Qutub Minar is a part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site. Qutub Complex is located within an ancient fort city named Lal Kot, which was founded by Tomar Rajputs of Delhi. The highest tower in India, it stands at a height of 72.5 metres (238 ft), with a width of 2.75 metres at the top and 14.32 metres at the base.

Built in red sandstone, Qutub Minar was initially built in 1199 AD by Qutbu'd-Din Aibak for the purpose of muazzin, calls for prayer. Later, three more stories were added by Shamsu'd-Din Iltutmish. The tower also received renovations during the rule of Firuz Shah Tughlaq and Sikandar Lodi. Later, in 1829, Major R. Smith also renovated Qutub Minar. You can see inscriptions in Arabic on the Minar and projected balconies with intricate carvings.

Other attractions that you must explore around Qutub Minar are Alai Darwaza, Iron Pillar, Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque and the Tomb of Adham Khan. Located in Mehrauli in South Delhi, it is easily accessible by public transport and private cars.

02 / DELHI

India Gate

One of the largest war memorials of India, it is located along Rajpath. A majestic archway standing in the middle of a crossroad, India Gate was designed by Edwin Lutyens. Its foundation was laid in 1921 by the then Duke of Connaught. This monument was built in honour of 70,000 soldiers who were killed during World War I. On the monument, you can also see the names of around 13,516 Indian as well as British soldiers who lost their lives in the Afghan war of 1919.

After independence, another memorial, Amar Jawan Jyoti (Flame of the Immortal Soldier), was added to honour the Indian soldiers who died during the Indo-Pakistan War of December 1971. This eternal flame had been burning since its construction. In 2019, a National War Memorial was built in the India Gate Circle near the National War Museum. Since 2022, Amar Jawan Jyoti has been housed within the National War Memorial. Around India Gate, there are lawns ideal for a picnic or relaxed stroll. It is also lit at night, making for a beautiful sight.

03 / DELHI

Red Fort

Located on the banks of the Yamuna River, Red Fort was built in 1639 by Mughal emperor Shah Jahan when the capital was shifted from Agra to Delhi. Built in Islamic architectural style, this impressive fort is primarily made of red sandstone, with majestic walls surrounding its imposing structure. It was from here that India's first Prime Minister, Jawaharlal Nehru, delivered his first Independence Day speech. Since then, it has been the site for hoisting the national flag on Independence Day.

Within the fort, you will find sections adorned with intricate carvings and designs. Major attractions include the Hall of Public Audiences, Drum House, Palace of Colour, Pearl Mosque, Hall of Private Audiences made of white marble, and Royal Baths. The evening light and sound show is another attraction not to miss.

Humayun’s Tomb

Recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, Humayun’s Tomb is the mausoleum of Mughal emperor Humayun. It was commissioned by his first wife, Empress Bega Begum, and designed by Persian architect Mirak Mirza Ghiyath and his son Sayyid Muhammad. The monument stands 47 metres high and 91 metres wide, combining Mughal and Persian architectural styles.

The tomb is surrounded by the famous Charbagh gardens. The complex is also the final resting place of other Mughal royals, including Bega Begum, Hamida Begum and Dara Shikoh. It is located close to Nizamuddin Dargah, another popular Delhi landmark.

05 / DELHI

Swaminarayan Akshardham Temple

“Akshardham” means the divine abode of God. It is regarded as an eternal place of devotion, purity and peace. Swaminarayan Akshardham in New Delhi is a mandir, a Hindu house of worship and a spiritual and cultural campus dedicated to devotion, learning and harmony. Hindu spiritual messages, vibrant devotional traditions and ancient architecture are reflected throughout its art and design.

The mandir is a tribute to Bhagwan Swaminarayan (1781–1830), the avatars, devas and great sages of Hinduism. The traditionally styled complex was inaugurated on 6 November 2005, with the blessings of HH Pramukh Swami Maharaj and through the efforts of skilled artisans and volunteers. It was built under the guidance of Pramukh Swami Maharaj of BAPS and celebrates India's cultural, traditional and spiritual history.

Spiritual Significance

Each element of Akshardham echoes with spirituality, including the mandir, exhibitions and gardens. The mandir has more than 200 murtis, representing spiritual figures across many millennia. Its spiritual premise is that each soul is potentially divine. Service to family, country, neighbours and all living beings can help a person move towards divinity, while prayer is a call to improve oneself and move closer to God.

A visit can be spiritually enriching through the messages of prayer, non-violence and the universal principles of Hinduism, as well as through the beauty of the mandir and its surroundings. The complex features a central temple, landscaped gardens, the Abhishek Mandap and the Garden of India. The musical fountain show, known as the Circle of Life, is a popular evening attraction.

Facts and Figures

  • Opened on 6 November 2005.
  • Constructed by Bochasanwasi Shri Akshar Purushottam Swaminarayan Sanstha (BAPS).
  • Inspired by HH Yogiji Maharaj (1892–1971 CE).
  • Created by His Holiness Pramukh Swami Maharaj.
  • More than 300 million volunteer hours went into making the complex.
  • Over 8,000 volunteers from across the world participated in its construction.
  • The mandir is built from intricately carved sandstone and marble.
  • Exhibitions on Hinduism include Bhagwan Swaminarayan's life and teachings, such as prayer, compassion and non-violence.
  • The campus includes open gardens, water bodies and a stepwell-styled courtyard.

06 / DELHI

Lotus Temple

A Baha’i House of Worship, Lotus Temple is not to be missed when you are in Delhi. This temple is known for its unique structure in the shape of a white lotus flower. It was built in 1986 and had seen more than 10 million visitors by 2014.

Made of white marble, its lotus-petal design was conceptualised by Canadian architect Fariborz Sahba. The structure represents a philosophy of harmonious living and respect for all religions. People from all religious backgrounds can visit and worship here. The nine pools around the lotus petals, which are lit at night, are also worth seeing.

Hazrat Nizamuddin Dargah

A Delhi trip is complete only when it includes a visit to the legendary Hazrat Nizamuddin Dargah. Dedicated to the Sufi saint Nizamuddin Chishti, this shrine is visited by devotees from many religions. Promoting the equality of all religions, the dargah is the final resting place of Saint Nizamuddin Chishti.

The mausoleum is built in red sandstone and white marble. Here you will also find the tombs of Jahan Ara Begum, daughter of Shah Jahan and Mumtaz Mahal, the revered poet Mirza Ghalib, and the Sufi poet and singer Amir Khusrow. The shrine is also known for its everyday evening qawwali sessions.

It is around 8 km from New Delhi Railway Station. The source lists Pragati Maidan and Indraprastha as nearby metro stations.

08 / DELHI

Birla Temple / Lakshmi Narayan Temple

One of the first large temples of modern India in Delhi, Birla Temple was built between 1933 and 1939 by industrialist Baldeo Das Birla and his sons. Spread over 7.5 acres, the temple has a beautifully maintained garden, fountains and a hall called Geeta Bhawan for discourses, along with the main temple dedicated to Lord Vishnu and Goddess Lakshmi.

The idols are made of marble brought from Jaipur, while the temple uses stones from Agra, Makrana, Kota and Jaisalmer. It was designed by Sris Chandra Chatterjee, considered a pioneer of the Modern Indian Architecture Movement. The temple is especially visited during festivals such as Diwali and Janmashtami.

09 / DELHI

Purana Quila

Built during the rule of Emperor Humayun and Afghan ruler Sher Shah Suri, Purana Quila is an old fort whose impressive structure has stood for centuries. Also known as Old Fort, it is surrounded by massive walls about 18 metres high. Its three arched gateways are decorated with white and colourful marble inlays and blue tiles.

The eastern gate, known as Bada Darwaza, is still in use. The southern gate is called Humayun Gate, while the third is known as Talaqi (Forbidden) Gate. The gates also feature Rajasthani architectural details such as jharokhas and chhatris.

Another major attraction is the Qila-i-Kuhna Mosque, built in 1541 in a pre-Mughal style. Sher Mandal, a double-storeyed octagonal tower begun by Sher Shah and completed by Humayun, is also worth noticing.

10 / DELHI

Gurudwara Bangla Sahib

A significant shrine among Sikh devotees, Gurudwara Bangla Sahib was built in 1783 by Sikh General Sardar Baghel Singh. The shrine is associated with Guru Har Krishan, the eighth Sikh Guru.

Once known as Jaisingh Pura Palace and owned by Raja Jai Singh, this was the place where Guru Har Krishan stayed in 1664. During an epidemic of smallpox and cholera, he served people from here and offered water from a well within the bungalow. A tank was later built by Raja Jai Singh; devotees often carry its water with them.

The complex also includes a hospital, library, Baba Baghel Singh Museum and a higher secondary school.
