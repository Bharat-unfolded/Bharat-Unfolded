01 · NALANDA, BIHAR

Nalanda University

Nalanda Mahavihara was established under Emperor Kumaragupta I in the 5th century CE. From the 5th to the 12th century, it grew into one of the most celebrated centres of learning in the world. Students travelled here from China, Mongolia, Tibet, Korea and other parts of Asia because of its international reputation.

The standard of teaching was exceptionally high. Students seeking admission were tested by teachers before they could study at the Mahavihara. Nalanda was a leading centre for Mahayana Buddhist learning, while subjects beyond Buddhism were also taught here.

At the end of the 12th century, the institution suffered destruction during the invasion led by Bakhtiyar Khilji. Monks were killed and the valuable library was burned. Today, Nalanda is remembered as a symbol of Asian intellectual exchange, and Nalanda University has been established nearby.

02 · NALANDA, BIHAR

Hiuen Tsang Memorial Hall

This memorial honours Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang), the 7th-century Chinese Buddhist monk and scholar who studied and taught at ancient Nalanda. Proposed to strengthen India–China cultural ties, its construction began in 1960 and was completed in 1984.

The Chinese-style complex features a statue of Xuanzang, a memorial column, a bell enclosure and artworks depicting his travels and Buddhist history. Allow around 45–90 minutes for a visit. Please keep the hall quiet and do not touch exhibits or artwork.

03 · BODH GAYA, GAYA, BIHAR

Mahabodhi Temple

Mahabodhi Temple in Bodh Gaya marks the place where Gautama Buddha attained enlightenment beneath the Bodhi Tree. Emperor Ashoka built an early shrine and the Vajrasana (Diamond Throne) here in the 3rd century BCE. The grand brick temple seen today largely dates to the 5th–6th centuries CE.

After centuries of repairs and periods of neglect, the complex underwent major restoration by Burmese rulers and British archaeologists in the 19th century, including work in the 1880s. Conservation and management later came under the Bodh Gaya Temple Act of 1949. The site is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

04 · GAYA, BIHAR

Shri Vishnupad Temple

Standing on the banks of the Falgu River in Gaya, Shri Vishnupad Temple is a major Hindu pilgrimage site, especially for Pind Daan and ancestral rites. Its sanctum holds a roughly 40 cm footprint, traditionally believed to be Lord Vishnu’s, set in rock within a silver octagonal basin. A pillared sabha mandap and tall pyramidal spire form part of the temple complex.

The present temple is widely credited to Queen Ahilyabai Holkar of Indore, who rebuilt or restored it in 1787 on an older sacred site. Pilgrims visit throughout the year, with especially large numbers during Pitru Paksha.

05 · RAJGIR, NALANDA, BIHAR

Vishwa Shanti Stupa

The Vishwa Shanti Stupa (World Peace Pagoda) stands on Ratnagiri Hill in Rajgir. Built by the Japanese Buddhist organization Nipponzan-Myohoji and inaugurated in 1969, the white marble pagoda has four golden Buddha statues representing birth, enlightenment, preaching and nirvana. The hilltop offers panoramic views of the Rajgir valley.

06 · RAJGIR, NALANDA, BIHAR

Griddhakuta Peak (Vulture’s Peak)

Griddhakuta Peak lies in the hills of ancient Rajgir. Its name means Vulture’s Peak, inspired by a distinctive rock formation resembling a vulture. Buddhist tradition remembers this as a place where Gautama Buddha spent rainy seasons meditating and teaching, with the Lotus Sutra and Heart Sutra associated with the site in Buddhist tradition.

The hill has ancient stone steps, rock-cut meditation caves and a small summit shrine. The climb is best planned in the cooler morning or late afternoon.

07 · RAJGIR, NALANDA, BIHAR

Brahm Kund

Brahm Kund is a sacred hot-spring complex in Rajgir, fed by seven underground streams. The main pool is known for its naturally warm water, reaching around 45°C. The springs are revered in Hindu, Buddhist and Jain traditions, and the stepped bathing kunds include separate areas for men and women.

08 · RAJGIR, NALANDA, BIHAR

Venu Van (Venuvana Vihara)

Venu Van is a peaceful bamboo grove and monastery complex near Brahm Kund. According to Buddhist tradition, King Bimbisara gifted this bamboo park to Gautama Buddha as an early monastic residence. The grounds include remains of a brick stupa, the central Kalandaka Tank and shaded walking paths for quiet walks and meditation.

09 · RAJGIR, NALANDA, BIHAR

Ghora Katora Lake

Ghora Katora is a scenic natural lake in a bowl-shaped valley surrounded by hills near Rajgir. Its name means “horse bowl,” and the lake features a large Buddha statue rising from the water. The quiet setting is popular for its hill views and boating.

Fuel-powered motor vehicles are restricted on the inner approach. Visitors usually continue from the drop-off point by e-rickshaw, tonga or bicycle.

10 · RAJGIR, NALANDA, BIHAR

Rajgir Wildlife Safari (Zoo Safari)

Spread across about 191 hectares between the Vaibhargiri and Songiri hills, Rajgir Zoo Safari lets visitors explore large natural enclosures in specially designed, closed and air-conditioned safari buses with trained guides. Its five zones feature lions, tigers, leopards, sloth bears and herbivores such as deer and blackbuck.

11 · RAJGIR, NALANDA, BIHAR

Rajgir Nature Safari & Glass Bridge

Set among the Vaibhargiri and Songiri hills, Rajgir Nature Safari is an eco-adventure park spread across a forest landscape. Its main attractions include India’s Glass Bridge skywalk, a suspension bridge, zip-line, sky biking, archery, rifle shooting, gardens and nature trails.

12 · PATNA, BIHAR

Buddha Smriti Park (Buddha Memorial Park)

Located on Fraser Road opposite Patna Junction, this 22-acre memorial park was built on the former site of Bankipur Central Jail and inaugurated by the 14th Dalai Lama on 27 May 2010. Its centrepiece is the 200-foot Patliputra Karuna Stupa, which houses sacred Buddha relics excavated at Vaishali. The park also features Bodhi tree saplings from Bodh Gaya and Anuradhapura, international votive stupas, landscaped gardens, a meditation centre, library and multimedia museum.

13 · PATNA, BIHAR

Bihar Museum

Located on Bailey Road (Jawaharlal Nehru Marg), Bihar Museum is a modern cultural complex designed by Maki and Associates with Opolis Architects. Opened in phases from 2015 to 2018, its galleries trace Bihar’s heritage from prehistoric times to the modern era. Highlights include the Mauryan-era Didarganj Yakshi, Children’s Gallery, History Gallery, Regional Art and Contemporary Art collections. In October 2026, it received international recognition in the Worth a Journey list of the world’s best museums.

14 · PATNA, BIHAR

Golghar

Near Gandhi Maidan and the Ganga, Golghar is a beehive-shaped granary built in 1786 by Captain John Garstin for the British Army after the devastating famine of 1770. The 29-metre-high structure has no internal pillars and is known for its exterior spiral staircase of 145 steps, originally designed for carrying grain. Its grounds and elevated viewpoint offer views of the river and Patna skyline.
